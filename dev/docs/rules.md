# **Ch1 \- What is Flow State?** {#ch1---what-is-flow-state?}

Welcome to Flow State, a Setting Universal, Digital Only, Open Ended Tabletop Roleplaying Game (SUDOOE-TTRPG, or Suhdewey-TTRPG)\! This rules doc here helps explain all the core concepts, rules, and systems of this game all in a simple and easy to digest manner. As this project is community assisted, this document will maintain a “Comments Allowed” feature that allows you to comment on anything within this document. If you use this feature, I ask that your comment is concise and useful, like for example if I have misspelled something or a rule is not entirely clear. With that out of the way, let's get into what Flow State is\!

## **SUDOOE** {#sudooe}

I know, this is a bit of a mess of buzzwords, but I’ll break down each of them to help you understand the pillars that hold this game up:

* Starting with Setting Universal, this is a term that effectively means that the rules and systems within Flow State are intended to be very fantasy forward, but otherwise simple and flavorless on purpose, so that the game can be run in any style of setting within the Fantasy constraint. Many parts of this system can also easily be removed, added upon, or altogether changed using the core framework to more easily fit whatever setting you want. What this means is simple, the game can be run in any setting so long as that setting has some simple Fantasy allowances, and a lot of the mechanics are written in ways that aim to keep things both simple and modular.  
* Post-COVID, a large number of TTRPGs were forced to be played online only. This led to a huge overarching market shift into digital only things, even well after the pandemic subsided. My game really takes advantage of that, with deep mechanical systems using complex math that, if done manually, would take a long time to set up. But thanks to the wonders of modern technology, everything is automated within a free to use Foundry VTT game system\! For players, this makes character creation simple and quick, while offering a ton of opportunity to learn and master the various complex mechanics, and for GMs this allows for a ton of minute tweaking to core gameplay rules to fit any kind of desired setting or system idea.  
* When I say Flow State is Open Ended, what I mean is twofold. On the Player end, Player Characters (PCs) can really go in any direction they want, there are no classes or restrictive systems. In addition, the amount of content in this game is meant to allow for any kind of concept to exist, at least in its basic terms. For example, Necromancy itself may not literally exist in this system, but Animation (magic tree) allows you to animate corpses all the same, and a player could easily flavor this as a necromancer as a result. For GMs, open ended means every system is modular, cleanly connected, and easily changeable, allowing for easy change and removal of everything.

## **Why make this?** {#why-make-this?}

Back when I was in college in 2017/2018, I played a ton of D\&D 5e, to the point where I started hosting my own games in my own unique setting. However, I found that I had a ton of difficulty molding certain features to fit my setting, and an even harder time creating new mechanics or ideas for players to mess around with without utterly breaking things. As a result, I spent a long time just fighting with the system to make it work how I wanted. After a while of this, I branched out into many other systems to find the Right One for me (World of Darkness, Powered by the Apocalypse, Fabula Ultima, Mutants and Masterminds, Warhammer Fantasy/40k, etc), but every time I always found pushback by the system to try and keep me in their setting or something adjacent to it. So, eventually I had enough and decided in 2019 to strike out on my own and make my own game, one where players and GMs alike could design and create anything using the structure provided by this system\!

## **So, how do I play?** {#so,-how-do-i-play?}

Well, just like most other TTRPGs, you need a GM and 3-5 Players to play, and they should be versed enough to guide you through the whole game process as well as character creation, but if you want to try and learn yourself read on in Chapter 2 and beyond\! If you are that GM, then hello\! Here are some useful things to know going into this; As I stated before, this game itself is Setting Universal. This means that I *highly suggest* coming in with your own fantasy setting (or one you are a fan of), and using this system as is for the first few sessions. Once you are used to running things a bit, you can begin removing or adding skill trees, changing the names of equipment, altering the stats of monsters, etc. When the GM guide is ready, I will link it here to help provide more direct tools to help shape this world how you like\!

## **Foundry VTT** {#foundry-vtt}

The primary way to play this game is the Foundry VTT game system, which can be found below in The Documents section (soon to be added). If you have issues with the module, let me know on Discord (@chris\_optymystical).

# Part One \- Making Your Character

# **Ch2 \- Ancestry** {#ch2---ancestry}

The first choice you make for your character is their Ancestry, which is the people they are born to (Humans, Dwarves, Orcs, etc). Your Ancestry decides a lot of the basics about your character before anything else, like their Size, languages, and lifespan, which can be found in that Ancestry’s Profile in Flow State \- ABCs. Each Ancestry also has its own skill trees that you can put points into like any other, but don’t worry about those yet, as skill trees are explained in Ch4. For now, pick the Ancestry that fits the character you have in mind. The most important thing it sets right now is your Size, which is explained below.

## **Size Categories** {#size-categories}

All creatures fall into a size category, which affects things such as how much space your character takes up, maximum movement speed, hunger rate, max HP, and melee attack range. All playable characters have a default size of 3 unless stated otherwise. Aside from what is listed below, your size also affects your Max Health, Pain Threshold, Grappling, and Carrying rules (see those sections for more details). Equipment has its own Size, which determines its power in place of yours (see Carrying in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

| Size | Space | Max Move Speed | Rations/Day | Unarmed & Natural Attacks | Melee Range |
| :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: | :---: |
| 1 | Under 1 ft cube | 10ft/AP | ¼ | Doubly Weakened | 1ft |
| 2 | 1-3 ft cube | 25ft/AP | ½ | Weakened | 3ft |
| 3 | 3-5 ft cube | 50ft/AP | 1 | Normal | 5ft |
| 4 | 5-10 ft cube | 100ft/AP | 2 | Strengthened | 10ft |
| 5 | 10-20 ft cube | 200ft/AP | 4 | Doubly Strengthened | 20ft |

# **Ch3 \- Archetypes** {#ch3---archetypes}

This game has its stats, skill trees, and equipment split into three distinct “Archetypes”, and everything within them is referred to as “Archetypal”. For example, a single dollar bill is not Archetypal equipment as it is used by everyone universally, but a Sword or a Gun is considered Archetypal as it is used by Martial Characters. To give some general context, each Archetype has its own list of skill trees, 3 associated stats, and unique archetypal equipment.

## **What are the Archetypes?** {#what-are-the-archetypes?}

In total, there are three Archetypes: Martial, Mental, and Magic. Each archetype has its distinct way of playing the game and interfacing with combat, but can host any general character role (tank, damage, support, debuffer, etc). When designing a character for the first time, it is highly recommended that you stick to just one Archetype to learn the game. Below are some brief descriptions of each Archetype to help you decide.

## **Martial Archetype** {#martial-archetype}

**Weapon Types:** The Martial Archetype is defined by their equipment, known as Weapon Types. Essentially, a Weapon Type is a general idea of how a weapon works in the real world, categorized into a single stat block. This means, as you design a Martial character, you build them around one or more Weapon Types that you may find interesting or suitable for your targeted playstyle, with each of the 14 Weapon Types having their own unique skill tree and two different weights, light and heavy.

**Armor Types:** Armor is broken up into 4 distinct weights; light, medium, heavy, and titanic. Each offers unique advantages and disadvantages compared to the others, and each has its own skill tree associated with it. You can only wear one armor at a time, and there is even a skill tree for if you want to play unarmored\! 

## **Mental Archetype** {#mental-archetype}

**Wonders:** Wonders are essentially ideas and emotions that, when focused on, can form into real tangible effects. Each Wonder is one end of an Axis of Reality, based on Religious/Spiritual Domains found a lot in fantasy settings (like Life/Death), with one end being a Dream and the other its antithetical Nightmare. Using Wonders requires having unlocked its associated Skill Tree, and each Wonder has a handful of Modes, which are the individual effects you Manifest. Manifesting a Wonder is much more open ended than casting a spell or attacking with a weapon, allowing you to choose custom ranges and enhancements as needed, but they are quite limited in what they can accomplish.

**Icons:** Icons are objects of belief or reverence that take on a named Form, which you Attune to in order to use. A Form grants a defensive Ward, as well as a Tenet: an extra effect tied to a Wonder of your choosing. 

## **Magic Archetype** {#magic-archetype}

**Spells:** Magic skill trees are made up of a Core Spell and several mods. The mods can be activated when you cast that Core Spell to enhance or alter its effects at the cost of resources, and you can also combine two different Core Spells into a unique Combo Spell, allowing you to use both of their mods.

**Foci and Shrouds:** Foci are an item that is used to cast your Spells. Not all Magic users require Foci to cast Spells, but generally Magic characters want a Foci to enable them to cast without requiring both casting stats. Shrouds on the other hand are like a veil attached to one's Spirit, which grants them defenses to various damage types, as well as other beneficial defensive effects. You can only ever attune to one Shroud and Foci at a time.

**Affixes:** Affixes are gems that you Attune to separately (they only need to be on your person) to enhance your Foci, Shroud, and spell casting. 

# **Ch4 \- Skill Trees/Groups** {#ch4---skill-trees/groups}

By this point you should have an Ancestry picked, and an idea of which Archetype you want to play. With all this finished, you may already have an idea of what direction you want your character to go, but have no idea how to actually get there. This section will both explain how skill trees and groups work, and help give you an idea of which ones to focus on.

## **What is a skill tree?** {#what-is-a-skill-tree?}

Skill trees in Flow State are a linear path of unlockable skills/features separated out by tiers. Each tier requires the previous one to obtain, and once unlocked, you permanently gain whatever features that tier gives.

## **What is a Group?** {#what-is-a-group?}

You’ve already been acquainted with the three Archetypes; Martial, Mental, and Magic. These are examples of Groups, which are essentially bundles of skill trees that have both a common structural tree (like the Theories for the Archetypes) as well as an overarching theme. Groups can be as small as just three skill trees, or as large as even 30 or more. Generally speaking, most characters will stick to just knowing 1-2 Groups until much further into a campaign, however you are not restricted in their acquisition in any way.  
Every Group is built from up to three kinds of skill trees. Its Core tree (an Archetype’s Theory, or a Generic Group’s namesake tree, such as Perception) binds the Group together: it defines what the Group is about, holds its base rules, and unlocks the rest of the Group. Spine trees (such as Mental’s Arts, Magic’s Arcanas, and Martial’s Methods) are optional supplemental trees that sit at the top of each tier, adding to how the rest of the Group works rather than introducing a focus of their own. Meat trees make up the body of the Group (such as Perception’s Sight, or Mental’s Wonders), and a Group can have any number of them. Each Group states what unlocks its trees, which is usually a tier of its Core tree.

## **The Theories** {#the-theories}

Each archetype has a titular Theory skill tree, outlining how the archetype itself plays out. In addition to this, each tier unlocks further skill trees for usage. So, if you want to use Brawling Methods in Martial, you would need at least Martial Theory at Tier 1, then you could pump Brawling Methods up as much as you can with your remaining points. The skills, actions, and passives found within each Theory offer both general rules to play that Archetype, but also features that can improve and define how you might play your character, so I highly suggest reading your respective Archetype Theory to understand both how to play, and what you can do at the top end.

## **Generic** {#generic}

Each Archetype's skill trees function exclusively within that Archetype, with specific abilities to help them synergize with the other Archetypes (Magic’s Weaving for example). The Generic Groups however are much broader with their applications. Generic Groups focus on things like Perception, Stealth, Persuasion, Deception, Energy, and many more concepts, and can be found in Flow State \- Generic Groups. Each Generic Group’s namesake tree has a free Tier 0 that every character has, holding the base rules for that group (such as Spot, Stealthing, and Sneak Attack).

# **Ch5 \- Background and Culture** {#ch5---background-and-culture}

## **Background** {#background}

Your Background is how your character was raised and what they did before the story began, picked from your Ancestry’s list of Backgrounds. If no Background fits the character you have in mind, the Outsider Background lets you build your own (see Outsider in Flow State \- ABCs). Since every starting character is a young adult just getting started in life, Backgrounds are the first step of a career (like a Recruit or an Apprentice Mage), not a seasoned veteran. Each Background grants 15 free points of starting skill trees, spent at normal tier costs (see Ch7), including any Theory tiers those trees need. These free points are typically placed for you automatically into a preset list of trees, occasionally with a choice or two along the way (such as picking a focus or filling a Trade Slot), all of which can be found in each Background’s entry in Flow State \- ABCs. Free points unlock trees and count toward their requirements, but toward nothing else, meaning they don’t increase your dice, your Stat Bonus, or your Energy. If a Background has points it can’t place, they go into your Ancestry’s trees instead.

## **Culture** {#culture}

Your Culture is the last part of your ABCs, and is made up of two parts: your Homeland, the people and place you come from (picked from your Ancestry’s list or the Shared Homelands), and your Circle, the group you came up in through your Background (picked from your Background’s list or the Shared Circles). Together, these set your character’s values, customs, outlook, and ties in the world. Cultures are purely roleplay and have no mechanical effect, but they give your GM hooks to pull your character into the world, so they are well worth reading.

# **Ch6 \- Stats** {#ch6---stats}

Each of the three Archetype stat groups have similar ways they are structured. For example: Constitution, Willpower, and Build all influence HP max and regen, and some form of defensive equipment’s power. Stats have no upper limit and no lower limit, and campaigns generally start you with 45 stat points (this can vary) which are spent 1 to 1 for stats (so you can start with 30 strength and 15 constitution if desired). Below is a detailed list of each stat, what it directly influences, and what its general use is within its archetype.

## **Martial Stats (Body)** {#martial-stats-(body)}

**Strength** \- Strength is a measure of how much force a character can put into each action they make as well as their overall focus. Affects Heavy weapon damage and effects.

**Dexterity** \- Dexterity is a measure of how quickly a character can physically react and act in combat. Affects Light weapon damage and effects.

**Constitution** \- Constitution is how much punishment a character can take, as well as how long they can last dishing out high power attacks. Affects armor stats and effects.

## **Mental Stats (Mind)** {#mental-stats-(mind)}

**Ponderance** \- Ponderance is a measure of how focused one can be on long term ideas or thinking. Affects Dream power and effects.

**Snappence** \- Snappence, inversely, is a measure of how quickly one can react to changes happening directly around them. Affects Nightmare power and effects.

**Willpower** \- Willpower is simply a measure of how strong one's mind is, and how resilient they are to negative change. Affects your Icon’s defensive Ward and its effects.

## **Magic Stats (Spirit)** {#magic-stats-(spirit)}

**Reach** \- Reach is a measure of how much energy one’s spirit can manipulate at a time. Reach affects your scaling with the Channeler casting form.

**Grasp** \- Grasp is a measure of how one understands and interfaces with magic. Grasp affects your scaling with the Igniter casting form.

**Build** \- Build is how energetic and tough one’s Spirit is, with more Build resulting in a denser Spirit. Build affects how powerful your Shroud is.

## **Modular Dice** {#modular-dice}

As you’ll see in the below sections, this system does not use standard dice for its rolling mechanisms and instead uses what is referred to as a “Modular Die” system. The reason for this is because it's a lot easier to backwards balance things when you never use modifiers for accuracy/checks, and additionally because Flow State is a digital only system, it can make full use of Modular Dice. Expect non standard dice to be everywhere, and Die Size to be referenced a lot (it simply refers to literally what comes after the “d”, so a die size of 15 is a d15).

## **Stat Checks** {#stat-checks}

Stat checks are rolled in response to various abilities or situations, and their die is a size equal to double your relevant stat. So having 30 Strength means you roll a d60 strength check. Assume you can make one stat check every few seconds to minutes depending on the time needed for the difficulty of the check.

**Stat Minimums:** Your Stat Minimum is 1/3rd of that stat, rounded down. Any time you roll a stat check out of combat (unless you’re in a time crunch), if your roll total is below your Minimum, it is set to your Minimum. Stat Minimums are also used in various skills and calculations, often read as Stat Min or “Stat Name” Min.

## **Advantage/Disadvantage** {#advantage/disadvantage}

Advantage and Disadvantage are stacking keywords that affect checks, attack rolls, and dodge rolls. They can stack infinitely, and one stack cancels out one stack of the other. When rolling with Advantage or Disadvantage, roll an extra die per stack, and use the highest (Advantage) or lowest (Disadvantage) for the result.

# **Ch7 \- Skill Points** {#ch7---skill-points}

## **How do I get skill trees?** {#how-do-i-get-skill-trees?}

Skill trees are improved using skill points, which are acquired whenever you would gain stat points. For every 3 stat points a character gains (through character growth), they also gain 1 skill point (known as the 3-1 stat-skill ratio). For most campaigns, players will start with 45 stat points, meaning they also start with 15 skill points.

## **How do I use Skill Points?** {#how-do-i-use-skill-points?}

Skill points are spent unlocking the various tiers of skill trees. Tiers cost an amount of skill points to unlock equal to their tier. In other words, to go from tier 4 to 5 in a skill tree, you would need to spend 5 skill points. To help visualize this, getting from tier 0 to tier 5 in *any* skill tree requires 15 skill points total. With your 15 starting skill points, this means you could easily pick one tree to put to tier 5 and call it a day. Also, if you don’t want to spend some leftover points, they can be banked away for future use, but still count towards your total skill points.

## **Total Skill Points** {#total-skill-points}

Your total skill points is the number of skill points you have earned, including any you have banked but not spent. The free points from your Background do not count toward it. This one number shows up all over the game, so here is everything it sets:

* **Attack Die:** Your attack rolls use a die equal in size to your total skill points (see Attacking/Dodging in Ch11).  
* **Dodge Dice:** Your dodge rolls use two dice, each equal in size to half your total skill points, rounded down (see Attacking/Dodging in Ch11).  
* **Non Stat Checks:** Checks like Perception, Stealth, and Persuasion roll the same die as your attacks (see Non Stat Checks below).  
* **Energy:** Your maximum Energy is based on your total skill points (see Energy in Flow State \- Generic Groups).  
* **Stat Bonus:** Every stat gets a bonus based on your total skill points (see Stat Bonus below).

So a starting character with 15 skill points attacks with a d15, dodges with 2d7, and rolls a d15 for Non Stat Checks.

## **Stat Bonus** {#stat-bonus}

In addition to any points invested in a stat, every stat a character has is increased by 1 for every 5 skill points they have. In other words, a baseline character with 15 skill points effectively has a floor of 3 in every stat.

## **Non Stat Checks** {#non-stat-checks}

Most checks that are not tied to any stat and are not attack/dodge checks are Non Stat Checks, such as Stealth, Perception, Persuasion, Deception, and Initiative. They are rolled using your plain attack die (a die equal in size to your total skill points), unaffected by effects that modify attack rolls unless those effects state otherwise. When two Non Stat Checks are contested, the acting side (such as the spotter or the persuader) wins ties. The rules for each of these checks are found in the Tier 0 of their group’s namesake tree in Flow State \- Generic Groups.  
When a Non Stat Check is not contested, the GM instead sets a static target number from the table below, and the check succeeds if its result is equal to or higher than that number.

| Difficulty | Target |
| :---- | :---: |
| Easy | 5 |
| Moderate | 10 |
| Hard | 15 |
| Very Hard | 25 |
| Heroic | 40 |
| Legendary | 60 |

# **Ch8 \- Finishing Your Character** {#ch8---finishing-your-character}

With your Ancestry, Archetype, Background, Culture, stats, and skill points all set, there are just a few last numbers to work out before you’re ready to play. If you’re using the Foundry VTT system, all of these are calculated for you, but it’s good to know where they come from.

**Health Points:** Health Points (HP) are a measure of how beat up and broken your character is, and are based on your Constitution, Willpower, and Build, as well as your Size. If your HP drops to 0, your character dies, so keep an eye on it (see Health Points in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

**Pain Threshold:** Your Pain Threshold is the point where your character falls unconscious from their wounds, and is based on your Max HP (see Pain Threshold in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

**Energy:** Energy is what all three Archetypes spend to use their abilities and actions. In combat it refills a bit each turn and you can spend AP to recover more, while outside of combat it is always full (see Energy in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

**Movement:** Your movement speed is how far you move for each AP you spend, and starts at your Size’s maximum (see Movement in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

**Starting Equipment:** Last, you get your starting gear. Most starting characters begin with a few pieces of common or uncommon equipment from their chosen Archetype(s), with the exact specifics being up to the GM (see Ch9 and Flow State \- Equipment).

Once that’s done, your character is ready to play\! Part Two of this document covers the rest of the rules, and is meant to be used as a reference while you play rather than read front to back.

# Part Two \- Rules Reference

# **Ch9 \- Equipment** {#ch9---equipment}

## **Archetypal Equipment** {#archetypal-equipment}

All equipment used in the three Archetypes is known as Archetypal Equipment. This includes Weapons, Armor, Icons, Foci, Shrouds, and Affixes. All equipment found within this system has a multitude of unique rules and mechanics behind them, all of which is located in the relevant [Equipment Document](https://docs.google.com/document/u/0/d/1bjinhIrPGJr0Ss4M9fjlCRSZrteX5r9yjsSuhYkMuag/edit). For most starter characters in standard campaigns, you will start with a few pieces of common or uncommon equipment of your chosen Archetype(s), with exact specifics being up to the GM.

## **Non-Archetypal Equipment** {#non-archetypal-equipment}

All things that do not fall explicitly into the Archetypal Equipment category instead fall here, and are generally treated or just referred to as “Objects”. There will be an expansive document detailing all of the various NA Equipment out there, but that all requires the ABCs, Gathering Professions, and Production Categories to be finished, so for now this section is empty.

# **Ch10 \- Exploration and Roleplay** {#ch10---exploration-and-roleplay}

## **Traveling Speeds** {#traveling-speeds}

When traveling, base party speed equals the slowest member’s speed, halved (rounded down). Using a vehicle grants you the positive effects of slow speed without the speed penalty. Choose one of three travel paces:   
**Fast:** \+50% speed, no downtime. 16 hours travel (8 rest).  
**Normal:** Regular speed, 12 hours travel (4 downtime, 8 rest), party is always aware.  
**Slow:** Half speed, 8 hours travel (8 downtime, 8 rest), party is always aware and can perform activities during travel.

## **Getting Around** {#getting-around}

Moving around the world uses the same Movement rules as combat, with rough and difficult terrain slowing you down. Physical feats like jumping, climbing sheer surfaces, and swimming against strong currents all have their own rules, and most use an Athletics check. How much you can carry before becoming Encumbered is covered by Carrying (see Athletics in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

## **Resting and Environments** {#resting-and-environments}

Characters must rest each day, and going without it has growing consequences. Resting is also when you recover HP. Harsh biomes like deserts and tundra (Extreme biomes) make travel and rest harder, and severe conditions like blizzards or sandstorms (Hazards) slowly drain your health while you are exposed without shelter (see Endurance in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

## **Senses and Stealth** {#senses-and-stealth}

Every character perceives the world through their senses (Sight, Hearing, and Smell by default), each of which can be Secondary, Primary, or Heightened, and light, darkness, and noise can all get in the way. To find something hidden, you use the Spot action to make a perception check against its stealth check. On the other side, anyone can begin Stealthing to avoid being noticed, and attacking from stealth gives you a Sneak Attack (see Perception and Stealth in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

## **Social Checks** {#social-checks}

During roleplay, you can try to Persuade or Deceive a target, making a persuasion or deception check against their counter check, with how friendly or hostile they are toward you changing the odds. A success never forces a target to act against its nature, and how they react is always up to the GM. Resisting persuasion, deception, intimidation, and forced mental effects like Fear and Charm all use counter checks (see Persuasion, Deception, and Resolve in Flow State \- Generic Groups).

# **Ch11 \- Combat Rules** {#ch11---combat-rules}

## **Turn Order** {#turn-order}

At the start of combat, all characters whether they are surprised or not make an initiative check (see Initiative in Flow State \- Generic Groups). When round 1 starts (after the surprise round of round 0, see Surprise in Flow State \- Generic Groups), all non-surprised characters get access to all of their RP, and can react just as combat is beginning if desired. From here, the person with the highest initiative goes, then the next highest, continuing until the final person has gone, then resetting back to the top for round 2 using the same scores. 

## **Action Points (AP)** {#action-points-(ap)}

Whether you're swinging a sword, casting a spell, or manifesting the essence of wrath to annihilate someone, you are spending AP (or RP) to do so. Every character has 6 AP, which refills at the start of their turn, and goes away at the end of their turn if it's unused. Each Archetype’s attack system (Martial have attacking, Mental have manifesting, and Magic have casting) follows the baseline cost of 1-3 AP to do their thing once. This means, the typical character can make 2-6 attacks per turn using their AP. Many other skills, actions, and even movement also cost AP, which will be listed in their respective sections. A final note, 1 AP is roughly equal to 1 second of time passage, with rounds being a total of 6 seconds long.

## **Reaction Points (RP)** {#reaction-points-(rp)}

Just the same as AP, every character gets 6 RP at the start of their turn, however it only goes away when you would start your turn again. This means that the RP persists beyond your turn, up until it's your turn again in which it goes away. RP are often used for interrupt based abilities that require some kind of condition to be met (like being attacked, being hit, an ally being attacked, etc), but not all Reactions are like this. Some can just be done at any time (such as a spell cast with the React mod for magic), in these cases you must declare the very moment they are occurring. When this happens, other characters may also react with their reactions, which will occur before yours, then once everyone is finished declaring, you follow down the chain of reactions until they're all finished. If a reaction is made invalid for some reason, it's treated as if it never happened, so no resources or RP are lost.

## **Strafing** {#strafing}

Strafing refers to moving while performing an action. So long as your Movement coincides with an Action that can be used within that Movement’s AP, that action can be performed using that same AP. In other words, you can strike someone with an attack using 2 AP, then with that same AP move backwards twice your movespeed.

## **Attacking/Dodging** {#attacking/dodging}

Attack rolls made by any character always have a die size equal to your total skill points, so starter characters would attack using a d15. Meanwhile, dodge rolls are made using two dice, each equal in size to half of your total skill points, rounded down, so a starter character would roll 2d7. Resolving a hit would be as simple as the attacker making an attack roll, the defender making a dodge roll, both roll totals being compared. If the attacker is equal to or higher, they hit and roll damage, otherwise they miss. If an attack is made in a situation where there is no duress (outside of combat for example), then the attack can automatically hit (a roll would be required to Crit).

## **Targeting** {#targeting}

Most attacks in this game are made against a single declared target. Unless otherwise stated, any objects or other targets in the way of your attack (such as armor, shrouds, walls, etc) must be broken through by dealing enough damage to get through their individual Limits (see Object rules for more info) before the next thing in line is hit. You can declare anything sensible as a target, from armor, to weapons, to even backpacks. If you would overkill a target, unless specified, any excess damage is lost. “Targeting directly” refers to targeting something that has nothing in the way at the time of the declaration.

**Allies and Yourself:** When you target an ally, willing target, or yourself with an effect, you must still roll your attack roll against that target’s dodge roll. You can still miss the attack as per the standard attack rules, and can still Crit.

**Objects and Empty Space:** An attack against an inanimate object, or against a point in empty space, automatically hits. Against objects, your attack roll is instead rolled to determine a critical hit, with rolls higher than or equal to the target’s Body (or Grade\*10 if it has no Body) being a crit. An attack against empty space cannot crit. An object being worn or held by a creature uses that creature's dodge roll instead. A projectile fired or launched from a source (such as an arrow from a bow) uses that source's attack roll as its dodge roll. This does not override effects that state their own required result against objects. 

## **Attack Source** {#attack-source}

This term comes up a lot in various skill trees, so here it is plainly. Every Archetype has its own “types” for attacking. Martials have Weapons, Mentals have Wonders, and Magics have Spell Schools. Each of these is considered an attack source, and anything within them that enhances those attacks or offers alternatives is considered a part of them. The Unarmed attack source is a catch all for both Unarmed strikes and Grapple attacks.

## **Damage Rolls/Types** {#damage-rolls/types}

Damage rolls themselves can never benefit from Advantage and Disadvantage, instead using the Strengthened/Weakened system which is below. In total, there are 7 damage types and 4 overall damage categories. These types have no meaning on their own (aside from Arcane and Supernatural), instead they are for both flavor and setup.

**Physical Category**: Entirely made up of just the Physical damage type, to keep things simple.  
**Elemental Category**: Made up of the Heat, Cold, Acid, and Radiation damage types, typically found as environmental effects usually with bonus effects (nothing innate).  
**Magical Category:** Made up mainly of Arcane damage, which is Weakened against targets that are not fully Magical and passes through (is not blocked by) non-magical things. Any damage produced by Magic is also Magical in addition to its own type (e.g. a Heat spell is Heat and Magical), allowing it to be blocked by protections that are either Magical or whatever type it is.  
**Supernatural Category:** Made up of the Supernatural damage type, dealt by Supernatural sources (such as Omens made with Esoteric Arts in Mental) and by Mental effects that drain the body directly. Supernatural damage passes through (is not blocked by) protections that are not specifically against Supernatural damage, except for Temp HP, Shielding, Shrouds, and Ward effects, which reduce it as normal. Damage from a Supernatural source that also has a normal damage type is treated as both, allowing it to be blocked by protections that are either Supernatural or whatever type it is.

## **Strengthened/Weakened** {#strengthened/weakened}

Damage dice cannot be rolled with Advantage or Disadvantage, instead they get the Strengthened or Weakened keywords, which can stack with no inherent limit, and one stack cancels out one stack of the other, happening before damage calcs. Strengthened stacks additively give the base damage \+50% increased damage, so three stacks would increase the damage by 150% (10 damage becomes 25 damage). Weakened, inversely, stacks multiplicatively, so three Weakened stacks goes 0.5\*0.5\*0.5, resulting in 0.125x damage (10 damage becomes 1 damage), rounding down with no minimum. In addition, Strengthened/Weakened stacks apply to numerical Bolded/Scaling effects found in the three Archetypes, such as Force applied, Slow/Haste stacks, etc.

## **Critical Hits** {#critical-hits}

To achieve a critical hit (crit), you must be attacking a target and roll double or more the result of the defender’s dodge dice for your attack result. Critical hits are doubly Strengthened (two stacks of Strengthened). Double Crits (happen rarely with abilities, and with Full Stealth later) give four Strengthened Stacks instead.

## **Temp HP and Shielding** {#temp-hp-and-shielding}

Some effects protect you with Temp HP or Shielding, both of which absorb damage before it reaches your health, but they work at opposite ends of the process.

**Shielding:** Shielding applies first, before anything else that would reduce or block damage, even before parrying with a weapon. Shielding is typically damage specific (such as Shielding against Heat damage), only blocking damage of that type, but if no type is listed it blocks damage of any type. Shielding does not stack unless it explicitly states that it does. If you would gain Shielding while you already have some, you keep whichever is higher.

**Temp HP:** Temp HP applies last, after every other reduction or block, right before damage would reach your health. Temp HP stacks, and it blocks everything, no matter the type or source. The only exception is effects that directly say you lose health (such as running out of breath while swimming, or Hazards), which are incredibly rare and skip Temp HP entirely.

## **Grappling** {#grappling}

Grappling is an involuntary capturing of one creature by another, typically requiring at least one free hand to maintain, and requiring you to be the same size or larger than the target you are grappling. While Grappled, a creature cannot move outside of the range of whatever is grappling them. The various ways grappling can be achieved are outlined in the Martial, Mental, and Magic documents. Once a creature is grappled, it can spend 2 AP to make a counter grapple check. This involves it making an attack roll against its grappler (who rolls dodge), with a success meaning it breaks free (some rules may vary by archetype, such as Magic’s Hold being rolled against a fixed value). If its grappler is an object, instead it makes that attack roll against the Limit of that object (they can also break the object to free themselves, if possible). Dragging a grappled target costs \+1 AP per move.

## **Pushing**  {#pushing}

Similarly to grappling, you can choose to instead simply push a target. This can only be done with a Martial attack roll, and instead of applying damage you impart an amount of force equal to 5x your Strength stat to the target (see Ch13 for more info on what Force does).

## **Cover** {#cover}

Cover is determined by if part of your body is open: if none of your body is open you have full cover; if up to half of your body is open you have half cover; if more than half is open you have no cover. While in full cover, attacks are treated as if the cover is in the way, in addition having Disadvantage on their attack rolls if the attacker cannot easily sense you, with the cover also being hit on a miss. While in half cover, only ranged and area attacks have Disadvantage hitting you, but the cover is no longer in the way, with the cover only being hit on a miss.

# **Ch12 \- Conditions** {#ch12---conditions}

## **Crouch/Prone** {#crouch/prone}

Crouching makes you 50% your size for hiding behind cover, and can be done at any time while it is your turn. While crouching you are treated as if in rough terrain, but have no other benefits or drawbacks. Being prone makes you count as 25% of your size for hiding behind cover, and can be done by spending 1 AP, with standing up also requiring 1 AP. While prone you have Disadvantage on dodging attacks unless you have some kind of method of dodging while prone, enemies have Advantage when attacking you in melee, and you are treated as if you are in difficult terrain.

## **Ignite** {#ignite}

Ignite is a stacking value that applies to an object or creature. At the end of that Creature’s turn (or at the start of the round for objects) it takes Heat damage equal to its Ignite stacks. Attempting to put it out requires 2 AP to be spent and can be done by any creature in melee range of the Ignited thing, which removes all Ignite stacks currently on them. If a target with Ignite stacks takes any amount of Cold damage, all Ignite stacks are removed from them. Ignite damage does not ignore armor, barriers, or temp HP.

## **Ignite Spread** {#ignite-spread}

*This rule is optional and is only intended for more realistic games.*  
Ignite spreads differently depending on if the afflicted target is attempting to put it out or not. If attempted, it does not naturally increase. Otherwise, it increases by 10% (rounded down) every turn unless it is greater than or equal to that target's Pain Threshold or Limit. If it is at or above that cap, instead it attempts to spread twice to surrounding tiles, with each spread requiring its own d8 roll to determine direction. Whether it's spreading to an ignited source or not, add Ignite stacks to that tile equal to 10% of the source’s stacks.

## **Stain** {#stain}

Stain is a stacking value that applies to an object or creature. At the end of that Creature’s turn (or at the start of the round for objects) it takes Acid damage equal to its Stain stacks. Removing Stains can be done by anyone in melee range (can also be the one who is Stained) who spends 3 AP, which instantly removes all Stains from that target. Stain damage does not ignore armor, barriers, or temp HP.

## **Slow/Haste** {#slow/haste}

Slow and Haste are opposing stacking values that apply to a creature, and cancel each other out equal to their values. At the end of a creature’s turn, it reduces its Slow or Haste stacks by half of its Pain Threshold, rounded down.

**Slow:** While a creature has at least half their Pain Threshold in Slow stacks, their movement costs are increased by \+1 AP. This grows to \+2 AP if their Slow stacks reach or exceed their Pain Threshold.

**Haste:** While a creature has at least half their Pain Threshold in Haste stacks, their movement costs are decreased by 1 AP (if already 1, they move twice as fast). If instead they have Haste stacks greater than or equal to their Pain Threshold, their movement costs are decreased by 2 AP (if already 1, they move three times as fast. If at 2, they cost 1 and they move twice as fast).

## **Charges** {#charges}

A Charge is an effect stored on a creature (or held by yourself, such as with Redirect), named after the effect that created it, such as a Decree charge. Unless stated otherwise, a Charge lasts until the start of its creator's next turn, does nothing until it is consumed, and can only be consumed by its creator when its listed trigger occurs, consuming at most one Charge of that name per trigger. Multiple Charges of the same name can be stored on the same creature, and consuming a Charge removes it.

## **Other Conditions** {#other-conditions}

Some conditions are defined in other documents, alongside the rules they belong to. Here is where to find each of them:

* **Surprised:** Initiative, in Flow State \- Generic Groups.  
* **Stealthing (Half/Full Stealth):** Stealth, in Flow State \- Generic Groups.  
* **Lightly/Heavily Obscured and Deafened:** Perception, in Flow State \- Generic Groups.  
* **Encumbered:** Athletics, in Flow State \- Generic Groups.  
* **Unconscious:** Pain Threshold, under Endurance in Flow State \- Generic Groups.  
* **Fear and Forced Mental Effects (such as Charm and Intimidate):** Resolve, in Flow State \- Generic Groups.  
* **Grappled:** Grappling, in Ch11.  
* **Concentration:** Mental Theory, in Flow State \- Mental Stage 1\.

Effects tied to a single skill tree, like Magic’s Poison or Martial’s Mark, are explained in that tree.

# **Ch13 \- Objects and Force** {#ch13---objects-and-force}

## **Objects** {#objects}

Anything that is non-living counts as an object, from armor, to animated objects, and even weapons. Summoned entities are considered creatures, not objects.

## **Body** {#body}

Objects have a Body score, which shows their overall size. Refer to the chart below to determine the other stats of objects based on their body.

## **Durability** {#durability}

The overall “health” of the Object. Reaching 0 or lower means the Object is broken and can no longer be used, but it is still fixable. Negative durability equal to or exceeding the normal max means the Object is completely destroyed.

## **Limit** {#limit}

How much damage the Object can take from each attack it blocks or attacks that are hitting through it. This is ignored if the Object is the target of the attack.

## **Force** {#force}

Force is a measure of how much kinetic force is being applied at any given time. Forced movement skips both steps below, instead going directly to Force damage. To resolve Force:

1. If the target is using Lift, subtract their Lift from the total Force. If the remaining Force is zero or lower, this process ends  
2. Subtract half their Max HP/Durability from the remaining Force unless they are falling via Gravity. Then divide the remaining Force by 10, rounded down. The result is the number of feet the target is pushed in the direction of the Force.

If Force applied is downward and is an amount of feet equal to or greater to that character’s height the target is knocked prone in addition to the damage dealt.

## **Force Damage** {#force-damage}

If a creature is pushed by Force into an object or another creature, they take Physical damage equal to 3 × the remaining untraveled distance (in feet). This damage is subject to reduction as normal. The struck object or creature takes the same amount of damage. If the pushed entity’s body would be destroyed during impact (for example, it can only take 400 more damage), then only that much damage is transferred to the object or creature it collides with, with the same working vice versa (continuing the travel afterwards if there is remaining force leftover).

## **Gravity** {#gravity}

Every setting has a Gravity value (G), which is the downward Force imparted to unsupported creatures and objects, representing the strength of that world's gravity. Standard gravity is 1, meaning Gravity equals your Max HP exactly**.** A GM may raise or lower G to model different worlds. A low-gravity moon sits at 0.5 G (or half Max HP), a crushing high-gravity world sits at 2 or 3 G, and zero-G sits at, literally, 0 G. 

## **Gravity Force** {#gravity-force}

When a creature or object is unsupported or falling, at the start of its turn (or at the start of a round for objects) it accrues downward Gravity Force (based on the Gravity of where they currently are), resolved through the Force system. This increases by the baseline Gravity Force each turn the thing is falling, capping out after a number of rounds based on Size, as seen below (known as Terminal Velocity):  
**Size 1:** 1 round, but takes no Force Damage from falling.  
**Size 2:** 1 round.  
**Size 3:** 2 rounds.  
**Size 4:** 3 rounds.  
**Size 5:** 4 rounds.

## **Flight** {#flight}

Any creature can fly so long as it has something providing it enough Lift. If you are flying downwards, your flight speed doubles. If you're generating Lift yourself and take damage equal to or greater than 1/10th of your Max HP or you are hit by enough Force to push you, you must spend 3 RP to stabilize yourself or you begin falling (see Gravity Force).  
**Lift ≥ ½ Your Current Gravity Force:** You fall slowly and safely when stabilized (no Force Damage from falling) at a rate equal to 10ft per round.  
**Lift ≥ Your Current Gravity Force:** Your flight speed equals your ground movement speed. You are not affected by downward gravity while stabilized (due to your lift being higher than the gravity force you would receive).
