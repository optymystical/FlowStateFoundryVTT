Martial balance review

What 42 simulated fights say about weapons, armor, the Martial trees and the monsters, before the Magic passes begin. Updated with 127 follow-up test fights.

42 fights · 354 rounds 7 runs (6 passes + a gap pass) System v0.75.18 23 Oct 2026
How the sim ran

Every fight ran live in the Test World through the real actions, chat cards and Action List, with an AI choosing attacks, options and defenses. The party is five characters on equal footing: 90 stat points and 30 Skill Points each, Common and Uncommon materials, Grade matching the stat. Bulwark is the tank, Warden the support, with Duelist, Breaker and Marksman. Each pass gave them the trees one Martial Theory tier unlocks (T1 through T5), taken up to T5.

They fought three groups: a Swarm of ten goblins (20 SP, 1/5), five Raiders with real gear (25 SP, 1/5) and an Ogre Tyrant boss (Size 4, 30 SP, 1/10). Monsters had no skill trees in any pass.

Summary

Nine findings

Look at

Two-handed Heavy weapons lead by a wide margin

Pooled over every pass, the four two-handed Heavy weapons are the top four in damage per AP: Heavy Bladed 2H 41.5, Heavy Swift 2H 19.0, Heavy Weighted 2H 16.9, Heavy Curved 2H 14.2. The party median is about 8. The Breaker carried one of these in every pass and out-damaged the rest of the party in five of seven runs. Heavy Bladed 2H's figure leans on free follow-ups (66 free next to 63 paid attacks), so it is the most inflated of the four.

Look at

Armor tiers jump at Heavy

Absorbed per hit: Light 4–13, Medium 10–17, Heavy 22–37, Titanic Iron 42. The Medium-to-Heavy step is the biggest, and it is enough to make a Heavy wearer close to immune to Light weapons that hit for 10–20. A Bulwark in Heavy Steel took no damage at all in some fights. Light Soft Leather (60 Durability) broke mid-fight more than once.

Look at

Free blocking for allies shut the monsters out in the T2 pass

With Defender and Heavy Armor trees, the party blocked for allies 159 times, plus Shield Toss, Perfect Block and Brace/Harden. The monsters dealt 62 damage across six fights, against roughly 2,000–5,000 in other passes. Free, unlimited ally Blocks are a settled ruling; this is the size of its effect, in case it matters to you.

Watch

Light Reach, Light Blast and Light Weighted trail

For the party, Light Reach managed 3.8 per AP, Light Blast 4.1 and Light Weighted 4.2. Light Reach hit 27% of the time: much of its volume is free follow-ups that miss, and its 9–18 damage per hit is mostly soaked. Heavy Defender (4.8) is low too, but it is a shield, so that is expected.

Watch

Rapid wins on volume; Assault's Solitary follow-up runs out of RP

Light Rapid hits only 44% (Pepper adds a Disadvantage), yet it scored the most kills of any party weapon (26) at 8.6 per AP. Heavy Assault's last follow-up could not fire for lack of RP 40 times. Three reloads at 2 RP each use up a whole turn's 6 RP.

Watch

Grappling locks a lone boss down completely

In the T5 pass, the party grappled the Ogre and held it with Lock Down and Disrupt. It spent its turns on Break Free and dealt 0 damage in both boss fights. A single large creature has no answer to it. Size limits help (weapon grapples take your size or smaller), but Unarmed grapples with Big Hands reach Size 4.

Watch

Defensive trees make fights longer, not safer

The T3 pass (Dexterity, Light Armor) used Shift 322 times and had the longest fights: 11.3 rounds on average, and one Raider fight ran 19 rounds with four of five party members down. Both sides' armor out-soaked the damage, and the fights turned into attrition.

Watch

Some abilities almost never fire

Riposte fired 2–3 times a pass with the T1 trees: it needs a fully stopped hit and spare RP. With Defender it fired 31 times. Omnislash fired once (it needs 4 Momentum on one target), Impale 2 times in 40 offers, Snipe Hunt 9 times. Part of this is the AI's own gating, so treat these as hints rather than proof. Limber is the opposite: 366 uses in one pass, and 255 more times it was wanted but unaffordable. It soaks up Energy.

Fine

Monsters without trees are no test for a party with them

The party won 39 of 42 fights. All three non-wins came in the baseline pass, before anyone had trees: one Raider loss and two boss fights that hit the 20-round cap. Once the party had trees, the goblins dealt 1–2 damage per AP into armor, and only the Raiders did real harm. This is the expected result, but it means passes 2–6 measure the party's trees against each other, not against a fair threat.

Follow-up tests · 23 Oct

What the focused sims found

Three runs after the review, 127 fights: mirror duels for 2H against 1H + shield (12), a kiting test for armor against speed (18), and a weapon benchmark against a dummy that only dodges (97: every type and weight, no trees, 4 rounds each, one fight per cell, so single cells are noisy).

Correction: Knockback damage was missing from the weapon tables

Driving a target into the ground deals Force damage on its own card, and the sim's weapon tables never counted it. Heavy Weighted, Thrasher and Heavy Unarmed are understated everywhere above. In the benchmark, scored by the HP the dummy actually lost, the Knockback weapons are the best against Heavy armor: Heavy Weighted 2H 34.4 per AP and Heavy Thrasher 2H 19.3, against a two-handed average of 4.7 for the rest. The sim now counts it.

2H vs shield

Two-handed won 9 of 12 mirror duels (1 went to the round cap)

Same Strength build, Weighted or Bladed at T5, with Strength T4 and a two-handed weapon, or Defender T4 and a Heavy Defender. Bladed: 2H won 5 of 6. A one-handed Bladed weapon loses its Solitary+ follow-up while anything else is held (Equipment doc), so the shield costs it its signature chain. The 2H side made 45–62 free follow-ups a duel, and its Parry stopped the shield user's hits. Weighted: the shield's Block soaked every 2H hit (0 to HP from hits), yet 2H still won 4 of 6 through Knockback Force, which goes around the shield.

Armor vs speed

Speed decides a fight against melee, because of Strafing

A Light Assault kiter (no trees) against a 2H Heavy Weighted chaser on a 400 ft field. Strafing lets the kiter shoot first and then walk back on the same AP, while the chaser's walk has to leave AP for its swing. A kiter at least as fast as the chaser won 10 of 12, mostly untouched. It lost only when the chaser won initiative, closed 85 ft and knocked it prone. A slower kiter lost 5 of 6. Heavy against Heavy: the kiter won both without a scratch. So armor's speed cost is real, and a melee build with no gap-closer (Dash, Leap, throws, Get Over Here!) can't touch an equally fast gun.

Light Reach / Blast

Light Reach is the rules; Light Blast was the AI

Light Reach did 4.7 per AP against Light armor, in the middle of the Light weapons (average 5.0): its d6 and Disadvantaged Solitary follow-up are simply low. Single-target Light Blast did 7.4, among the best Light weapons. Fired as an Area into three clustered dummies it did 5.5 in total: the Weakened damage is mostly soaked by armor. The passes' low Light Blast number came from the AI choosing Area whenever two foes stood close. Area only pays off against unarmored crowds.

Assault + Quickload

Quickload now covers the empty Solitary follow-up

Your ruling is in (v0.75.19): an empty gun's free follow-up can be given up to reload at no cost. In the benchmark, Heavy Assault with Quickload turned its 3 failed follow-ups per 4 rounds into 2 free reloads and 1 extra shot, and started each turn loaded.

Rapid

Rapid without its tree barely hits

With Pepper's Disadvantage and no Rapid tree, Rapid hit 8–13% against a Dex 30 dummy. Light Rapid managed 0.7 per AP. In the passes it did well only with the Rapid tree, whose Quickload shot removes a Disadvantage.

What the numbers say

  • Two hands on a Heavy weapon roughly doubles output over the one-handed version: Heavy Weighted 7.5 vs 16.9 two-handed, Heavy Curved 7.0 vs 14.2.
  • Light Curved (13.7) is the best one-handed weapon, mostly from Momentum stacks in the T4 pass.
  • Unarmed holds up well with its trees: Light 13.3, Heavy 10.1, with a 79% hit rate on Heavy.

Read with care

  • A weapon on a stat under 10 rolls no dice (settled ruling), so the AI skipped those weapons.
  • Monster weapon numbers come from tree-less monsters hitting armored characters, so they are low across the board.
  • Users differ in stats and trees between passes; a weapon's pooled number mixes those builds.
Armor

Absorbed per hit, all passes pooled

Damage soaked per hit that reached the armor

Armor with 20+ hits

Scale 0 to 41.9. Limits set these numbers more than material does: Heavy Iron and Heavy Steel sit close together, and well above any Medium armor.

Light armor's value is speed and stealth, not soak: at about 6 per hit it barely changes a fight, and its low Durability means it can break before the fight ends. Medium sits in a narrow band (10–17). If the gap to Heavy is intended, the sim confirms it is large; if Medium is meant to be a real middle ground, it currently isn't.

Monsters

How the three groups fared

Swarm

Ten goblins at 20 SP (buffed from eight at 15 after the baseline). Their Light weapons hit 18–27% and did 1–1.4 damage per AP against the party. They only threaten through numbers on an unarmored character.

Raiders

The only real fight. They won one baseline fight and took four of five party members down in a T3 fight. Heavy Rapid and Heavy Swift 2H did the damage; the Heavy Blast bomber hit 10% of the time.

Ogre Tyrant

Strong before trees: two of three baseline fights went 20 rounds with the Ogre at about 30% HP. With trees it fell in 5–11 rounds. Grappling (T5) and ally Blocks (T2) shut it down. Its Light Striker natural weapon did 1.5 per AP.

Before Magic

Questions for the designer

  • Two-handed Heavy weapons. Is roughly double the one-handed output intended, given the shield or off-hand weapon you give up?
  • Medium armor. Should it sit closer to Heavy, or is the big step at Heavy the point?
  • Light Reach and Light Blast. Their damage per hit is too low to get through armor. Is that their role (reach and areas), or do they need more?
  • Assault's Solitary follow-up. Three reloads use the whole 6 RP, so the last follow-up rarely fires. Intended?
  • Grappling a lone boss. Should large or boss creatures get a better way out of Lock Down?
  • Monster trees. For the Magic passes, should the monsters get trees too? Without them, every pass will look like a party sweep.
