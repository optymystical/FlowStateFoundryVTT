Magic balance review

Five passes, one per Magic Theory tier: pure casters against caster monsters built from the same schools. What won, what did the damage, and what looks off.

30 pass fights, 12 re-run, 94 benchmark 5 passes (T1–T5) Pain Threshold ½ 24 Oct 2026, updated after the re-runs (v0.80.3)
How the sim ran

The same five-character party as the Martial passes (90 stat points and 30 Skill Points each), now as pure casters: Magic trees only, an attuned Foci and Shroud, armor, no weapons. Bulwark is the tank, Warden the support, the others casters on Grasp, Reach or both (the Raw caster has no Foci). Each pass took the schools one Magic Theory tier unlocks up to T5. The Swarm (ten goblins), Raiders (five) and Ogre Magus boss cast from the same schools, scaled to their Skill Points.

The AI casts through the real Cast Spell path: one support spell a turn (Shield, Burden, Repulsion), then its best spell per AP on the weakest foe in range, adding Mods while the Energy lasts. Tier 3 adds Poison coats, Charm and Hex; Tier 4 keeps one creation up each turn; Tier 5 adds Restore, Mirage and Shift.

Where Magic stands

Findings and what happened to them

Changed

Animation Size is capped by the Threshold (v0.80.0)

An Animation's health is material HP per point × points × Size, and its Size came from the pile, which had to hold at least the points, so Size grew with the points. The first T4 run had Stone Animations at 2,643 HP. Now Size follows the pile but needs Threshold 5 for Size 3 and 10 for Size 4, as a Summon's does, and there is no Size 5 (Magic doc and code).

T4 re-run: Animations average 2,042 HP and the party still wins 4 of 6, in 4.0 rounds. They remain about six times a Summon: Make + Animate gets 10 points per Threshold (Animate gets 5), so a Clay one reaches about 2,250 HP at Size 3. The points are the next lever if they still feel too strong.

Fixed

Restore's window and stacking (v0.79.5), Renewal too (v0.79.6)

Cast on your own turn, "since the start of your last turn" now reaches back to your previous turn (never before the fight began), and health Restore or Renewal already gave back counts against the window. The heal itself (2 × Power) stays small by design: health damage is meant to be deadly, and Restore is for patching up after a fight.

Sim fix

Venomancy's Poison never passed on in the first T3 run

A coating passes on through its Pass the Poison button, which the sim never pressed, so the first T3 pass (0 of 6) measured Venomancy as nothing. With the button pressed, the T3 re-run won both boss fights (2 of 6, 7.2 rounds): Cut + Poison and Stab + Poison ignore armor and took 806–1,249 HP off the Ogre Magus, which the carrier spells alone barely scratched. The swarm and raider fights are still losses.

Fixed

Summons are fully Magical (v0.80.2)

A Summon is made of Spirit, so Arcane isn't Weakened against it and Strike has Advantage. Animations stay non-Magical. Neither is a living target: Restore, Cut's living die and Mental's living-target checks skip both.

By design

Tier 2 fights are won by stacks

In T2 the party lost 8,078 HP and only about 1,940 of it was direct spell damage: the rest was Ignite, Stain and Electric Stain burning. Stacks are meant to matter more as effects than as damage, so this is the intent.

By design

Strike is strongest against armor

Arcane passes through non-magical armor and Strike is Targeted (Advantage), so against Heavy armor it out-damages everything (about 56 a cast, 88% hits). Against unarmored foes the Weakened stack puts it at parity with the Tier 1 Cores. Shields, Shrouds and magical materials answer it in play.

Kept

Venomancy is held back by armor, and Poison stays as written

Against unarmored raiders Venomancy keeps up with the best (about 904 HP in 4 rounds); against armor it lags (about 146 over 9 fights), because its carrier must deal direct damage before the coating passes and the targets pass most Constitution checks. Three variants were tested (below); a passed check halving the damage was the only one that helped against armor. You chose to keep Poison as written.

Watch

Squishy casters die before they act

Monsters go for the lowest HP in range, and the Raw caster (Con 30, Light armor, no Foci) often went down in round 1 before its first turn. A pure caster party has no one to hold the line except Shields and creations.

Watch

Combos lead Tier 1; burns and afflictions go uncredited

Force + Cut averaged 42.4 per cast against 20.7 for Cut alone. Poison ticks, Hex and Charm damage and stack burns land on their own cards, separate from the spell that caused them, so at the table it's hard to see what a Venomancer or a Heat caster contributed.

Fine

Bosses are beatable, as intended

With the re-runs the party won all 10 boss fights outside the first T3 run, in 2–10 rounds.

Read with care

Sim judgment calls and gaps

Choices the sim made

  • T3 carrier school: every T3 caster spent its last 3 SP on Cut, Stab or Slam at T2, so Poison had something to ride on.
  • Restore's window: the start of the caster's previous turn, not the current one.
  • Creations: one two-handed Heavy natural weapon for Arm, Skin at Medium, Strength-heavy points, the best Heavy weapon Make allows.
  • Shift: material always at hand; Harden (Strengthened) and Muddy (their attacks at Disadvantage) added on when affordable.
  • Mirage: Mirage + Strike when known, otherwise Fidelity for Disadvantage on attack rolls. Aura was left out (it would catch allies).

Gaps in the numbers

  • Burns and afflictions aren't credited to a spell; they show as HP lost outside direct hits.
  • T1 and T2 can't split spells by side (both sides used the same ones), so their table is pooled.
  • The AI spends AP on afflictions even when they do little (Charm on an Ogre that never attacks), so T3's damage is a floor. Until the re-run the sim never pressed Pass the Poison.
  • A harmless server error (an update to a token that was already gone) showed up in a few fights; none changed the outcome.
Decisions

The questions, answered

  • Restore's window and stacking: on your own turn the window starts at your previous turn; heals count against it (v0.79.5, Renewal v0.79.6).
  • Restore's size: small by design. Health damage is meant to be deadly.
  • Animation health: Size capped by the Threshold like Form, no Size 5 (v0.80.0).
  • Tier 2 stacks: meant to matter more as effects than as damage.
  • Tier 3 damage: Charm is meant to be weak (mostly out of combat); Witchery is strong; Venomancy is held back by armor and Poison stays as written.
  • Arcane: strong against armor is its intent; shields, Shrouds and magical materials answer it.
  • Summons: fully Magical; Animations aren't; neither is living (v0.80.2).
